# Research abstracts

Use for drafting, revising, or reviewing a research abstract. Follow the supplied
venue requirements, structured headings, and word limit before these defaults.

## Content selection

- Open with the concrete research problem and why it matters to the intended
  reader. Name the affected activity or decision. General claims that a topic is
  growing or important do not establish motivation. Strengthen an existing
  motivation when needed rather than declaring it absent without checking.
- Explain the approach and the mechanism needed to understand its contribution.
  Select the relevant mechanism rather than listing every feature.
- Include the most informative measured results available. Prefer outcomes that
  answer the research question over counts of tests or implementation components.
  For a tool demonstration, regression counts and observed protocol behavior may
  support implementation validation; they do not establish user benefit or
  effectiveness on real workloads.
- State what each number measures, with its unit, denominator, comparison, and
  experimental setting where needed to interpret it. Use only reported evidence.
  Do not invent percentages, combine overlapping test counts, or imply that more
  tests establish stronger real-world effectiveness. If quantitative results are
  unavailable or inappropriate, report the supported qualitative contribution.
- End with the contribution supported by those results. Follow venue instructions
  for availability or demonstration links. Avoid generic claims of significance.

## Framing and scope

- Describe what the method does and what the evaluation found. Remove repeated
  defensive statements and lists of capabilities that were not evaluated.
  Preserve negative or null findings when they are substantive results, and use
  negative wording when it states the problem accurately.
- Keep detailed limitations and future work in the body by default. Retain a
  limitation in the abstract when the venue requires it or its omission would
  materially mislead the reader. Do not apply a blanket ban on limitations.
- Keep essential scope next to the result: for example, a scripted fixture, a
  simulation, a fixed callback, or a small observational sample. Removing a
  disclaimer must not turn local validation into a claim about autonomous
  operation, causality, productivity, or general effectiveness.
- State comparative outcomes fairly, including parity when relevant. A blocked
  task is not a successful repair; a planning result is not an executed outcome;
  fewer executed checks alone do not establish lower total cost.

## Revision evidence

For each proposed change, identify the original passage, the specific issue, and
the evidence supporting the replacement. Trace every abstract result to the body
and retained data using the skill's Claim-Evidence Map. Distinguish required fixes
from stylistic preferences; reviewer feedback is not a universal venue rule.
When updating a submission, keep the manuscript and portal abstracts consistent
within the user's authorized scope and verify the final word count.
